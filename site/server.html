A Choice for Compute Independence

Home-labbing

Fifteen years of a homelab, told in photos: four homes since 2011, and two matching 30U racks, collected together around Smyrna and Marietta, Georgia, in a Home Depot rental truck. One still runs the lab; the other now holds the batteries for the powerwall. Every photo is in the datacenter gallery, and the current setup is at the bottom.

Georgia 2011 – 2014

The lab started in Georgia: one enclosed rack, a KVM drawer and prometheus, a Dell PowerEdge running VMware vSphere, later replaced by OpenStack across a fleet of second-hand Dells. By 2014 it was also running USB bitcoin miners, just before the move to Florida.

Ormond Beach apartment 2014 – Jul 2015

First stop in Florida was a rental apartment in Ormond Beach. The 30U rack lived in a hallway closet, Supermicro and Dell servers stacked under purple Extreme Networks switches.

Daytona Beach rental Jul 2015 – Sep 2019

Four years in a Daytona Beach rental, and the busiest stretch in the photos: storage builds, three Atom cluster nodes lost to one Intel bug, a pegboard network wall and a short-lived RGB phase. It ends with a flight case bought for one job, moving the lab.

Ormond Beach, today Sep 2019 – now

Back in Ormond Beach, this time in a house. The big rack settled into a closet with a wall cabinet above it. A three-NUC minilab came next, bought on eBay in June 2022 and racked in an 8U 10-inch rack; it ran the cluster as k3s in 2025 and has since been handed down to my son. In 2026 the Minisforum refresh moved the cluster to three Mini-ITX nodes in a 12U 10-inch rack, which now run entirely on 10 Gb fiber.

The software

Four homes, three platforms. VMware vSphere came first, on prometheus, a Dell PowerEdge, through version 5.5. Still in Georgia, OpenStack replaced it, spread across Dell PowerEdge R510, R610 and R710 servers picked up for $150 to $500 apiece when a former employer shut down a data center. Kubernetes arrived in 2015, starting around 0.9 (1.0 shipped that July), and ran on top of OpenStack until 2016 or 2017, when it went bare metal on the same Dells, built by hand following Kelsey Hightower's Kubernetes the Hard Way. In 2025 it moved to k3s on the NUC minilab, and in 2026 onto the Minisforum nodes in the 12U mini rack; the big rack now only keeps storage online. The first of those nodes, vulcan, started out as a standalone AV1 encoder, running a video-processing script that dates back to April 2025. In May 2026 that script grew into the Video Ingestion System, a Kubernetes-native distributed encoder that now runs on the cluster.

What it runs on today

  • Edge - OPNsense on a Deciso DEC2700, with two internet uplinks: fiber and satellite. The firewall and the fiber both arrived in May 2025, five days apart
  • Compute - a three-node k3s cluster on bare metal (vulcan, worker-02, worker-03), on Minisforum BD795i SE Mini-ITX boards: Ryzen 9 7945HX (16 cores, 32 threads), 128 GB of DDR5 (the spec sheet says 96) and a 2 TB NVMe each. vulcan's board came first, in August 2025, and spent its first months as a dedicated video encoder; the other two arrived at the end of April 2026
  • Out-of-band - a JetKVM per node, mounted in the 10-inch rack above them
  • Network - an all-fiber 10 Gb SFP+ fabric; each node bonds two Intel 82599 ports into 20 Gb. Ubiquiti EdgeSwitches and UniFi cover the rest of the house
  • Storage - gringotts, a Dell PowerEdge R730xd running TrueNAS SCALE, serving iSCSI and NFS to the cluster
  • Delivery - Flux GitOps; this site is built into an image and served from the cluster
